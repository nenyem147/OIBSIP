HER STORY

A Monument That Inspires the World

The Statue of Liberty, officially known as Liberty Enlightening the World, is one of the most recognizable monuments on Earth. Located on Liberty Island in New York Harbor, she stands as a powerful reminder of freedom, democracy, and the human desire for a better life.

The statue was a gift from the people of France to the people of the United States. It was conceived as a symbol of friendship between the two nations and a celebration of liberty. French sculptor Frédéric Auguste Bartholdi designed the monument, while engineer Gustave Eiffel helped create its remarkable internal framework.

Made primarily of copper, the statue features a raised torch in her right hand and a tablet in her left. The tablet carries the date July 4, 1776, marking American independence. At her feet lie broken chains, representing freedom from oppression.

Beyond its impressive architecture, the Statue of Liberty has become a symbol of welcome and opportunity. For generations of immigrants arriving through New York Harbor, her illuminated torch represented the possibility of a new beginning and a brighter future.

A JOURNEY THROUGH TIME

Her Historical Timeline

Important moments that shaped an enduring symbol.

1865

The Original Idea

French political thinker Édouard de Laboulaye proposed creating a monument celebrating liberty and friendship between France and America.

1875

Construction Begins

Work on the monument began in France under the direction of sculptor Frédéric Auguste Bartholdi.

1884

Completed in France

The completed statue was formally presented to the United States in Paris.

1885

Arrival in America

The statue arrived in New York Harbor in pieces aboard the French ship Isère.

1886

Grand Dedication

On October 28, the Statue of Liberty was officially dedicated, beginning its journey as a worldwide icon.

1986

Centennial Celebration

The monument celebrated its 100th anniversary following a major restoration project.
